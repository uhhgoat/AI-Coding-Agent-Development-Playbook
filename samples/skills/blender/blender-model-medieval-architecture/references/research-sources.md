## Instructional and process sources

- [Behind the Scenes: Medieval House](https://www.blendernation.com/2020/12/04/behind-the-scenes-medieval-house/)
  documents a low-poly stylized house built after a large PureRef study. The
  process proceeds from walls and roof ridge to separate roof tiles, stairs,
  ground, props, procedural wood, painted ground, lighting, and day/night
  renders. Transferable lesson: simple-looking low-poly scenes still require
  deliberate reference, placement, and composition work.
- [Modular Lowpoly Medieval Environment](https://www.blendernation.com/2022/03/20/modular-lowpoly-medieval-environment/)
  demonstrates modular tiles for medieval houses, Krita-authored textures, and
  offers downloadable Blender files. Verify the linked download license and
  current terms before inspection or reuse.
- [GameDev.tv: Blender Environment Artist](https://gamedev.tv/courses/blender-environment-artist-5)
  teaches a modular castle, corridor, outdoor environment, medieval house,
  reusable game assets, PBR materials, and Asset Browser organization. It
  explicitly distinguishes quick and detailed modeling methods.
- [3D Tudor: Blender and game-art training](https://3dtudor.com/)
  provides modular environment, dungeon, architecture, Geometry Nodes, and
  material courses and tools. Use the original course or product page for a
  specific workflow; do not infer unseen lesson content from a storefront
  image.
- [Medieval House Breakdown by Finn Bogaert](https://www.artstation.com/artwork/y4vyAO)
  shows a reference board, modular kit, tileable materials, packed masks,
  decals, repeated sculpt sources, and alternate seasonal surface states.
  Transferable lesson: modularity and high material quality can coexist when
  unique parts are limited.
- [Modular Castle Asset Pack Breakdown by Alan Aldred](https://alanaldred.artstation.com/projects/w8WZ19)
  shows Blender blockout, modular piece layout, a trim sheet, doors/windows,
  and engine assembly.
- [Creative Bloq: Building a World through Narrative Design in Blender](https://www.creativebloq.com/3d/3d-art/building-a-world-through-narrative-design-in-blender)
  emphasizes reference libraries, large-shape and hero-structure blockout,
  modular bases/pillars/windows, named collections, functional town zones,
  audience journey, and material contrast.
- [Blender Manual: Asset Libraries](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/files/asset_libraries/introduction.html)
  defines reusable Blender assets and library organization.
- [Blender Manual: Array Modifier](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/modifiers/generate/array.html)
  is the authority for exact repeated modules and local-scale behavior.
- [Polygon Runway: Stylized Roof Tutorial in Blender](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UajXnhYsGbo)
  combines Array, Simple Deform, and Lattice for a stylized roof. The adopted
  lesson is the separation of repeat generation from coherent whole-roof
  deformation; the included sample cottage shingles and fixture script were
  authored independently.
- [Blender Manual: Brick Texture](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/render/shader_nodes/textures/brick.html)
  documents row offset/frequency, squash, mortar, width, and row-height
  controls. These are useful for distant or baked course signals, but do not
  replace hero-distance shingle overlap geometry.

## Findings adopted by this skill

- Reference boards should contain both style benchmarks and real construction
  evidence.
- A building begins with footprint, large masses, roof, openings, and scale,
  not decorative boards or bricks.
- A reusable kit needs an asset zoo and alternate assembly, not only one hero
  scene.
- Repetition should use shared sources and a controlled variant set.
- Asset Browser catalogs are a natural endpoint for reusable Blender modules
  and materials.
- Tileable materials, trims, packed masks, decals, and a small number of
  sculpt/bake sources can carry quality without unique geometry everywhere.
- Geometry remains necessary where roof, damage, battlement, or beam detail
  changes the silhouette.
- Visible roof coverings need explicit drainage direction, overlap, staggered
  seams, and deliberate termination at the eave/ridge/end.
- Structural-frame review must include door/window clearance; a brace that
  crosses glass is not made plausible by otherwise coherent timber rhythm.

## Rights and quality rules

- Record author, URL, license, retrieval date, and file checksum for every
  downloaded project.
- Open downloaded `.blend` files with auto-execution disabled and inspect them
  through `blender-inspect`.
- Do not use `NoAI` assets for automated reconstruction or skill derivation.
- Do not redistribute paid or marketplace project files.
- Prefer creator, course, official documentation, and primary breakdown pages
  over mirrors or summary sites.
- A fantasy tutorial is workflow evidence, not historical evidence. Add
  architectural surveys or conservation sources when period accuracy matters.
